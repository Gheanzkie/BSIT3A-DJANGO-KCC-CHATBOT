--- 2.5 A BRIEF HISTORY OF KABANKALAN CATHOLIC COLLEGE, INC. ---
(Formerly Kabankalan Academy)

1927 - Founding as Kabankalan Institute
  In 1927 a group of incorporators gathered to establish a secondary school
  named Kabankalan Institute which was later changed to Kabankalan Private
  Academy.

1940-1946 - First Commencement & The War Years
  The first commencement exercises of the school was in 1940. Classes were
  suspended during the Second World War (1941-1944) and were resumed in the
  school year 1945-1946. The Board of Trustees got a permit to open a Junior
  Normal College which lasted only for three years due to poor enrollment.

1953 - Columban Fathers Acquire the Academy
  In 1953, the Columban Fathers through Fr. Thomas J. Cronin, parish priest
  of Kabankalan, thought of putting up a new school but later negotiated to
  buy Kabankalan Academy. However, most of the stockholders donated their
  shares in exchange for scholarship privileges.

Columban Era - Transfer to the Church Site & Directorship Practice
  When the Columban Fathers took over, the first decision they made was to
  transfer the site of the school to the space near the church. The school
  directorship under the Columban Fathers was not a permanent position. The
  change of the directorship went with the reshuffling of the parish priest.
  This was the standing regulation among the Columban Fathers.

1963-1964 - College & Elementary Departments Open
  Through the untiring efforts of Fr. Cronin, a general college and an
  elementary education was opened in the school year 1963-1964. Two years
  after, he was able to get the services of the St. Paul Sisters of Chartres,
  to assist in the management of the school. Their experience in school
  management was an asset. Through them, Kabankalan College became the
  center of learning in the southern part of Negros Occidental.

1983 - Diocesan Administration Begins
  The College department was built on a hectare of land donated by the Zayco
  family. In 1983 the management services of the Sisters of St. Paul
  terminated and the school administration shifted to the hands of the
  Diocesan priests headed by Msgr. Josefino I. Iledan as President and
  assisted by the Presentation Sisters.

1987 - The Great Fire & Reconstruction
  On March 14, 1987, while the town was celebrating its 80th Foundation Day,
  a fire of dubious origin razed the high school building, the parish convent
  and the Old Catholic church to the ground. The catastrophe brought untold
  financial losses to the school. Massive rehabilitation and reconstruction
  were immediately instituted as the school faced the opening of the next
  school year. Meanwhile the high school department was temporarily relocated
  to the College campus while the permanent three-storey building on the
  newly acquired lot adjacent to the College was under construction.

Campus Expansion - St. Columban Arts Center & Annex
  An additional phase to the physical structure of the college is the
  St. Columban Arts Center which serves as the socio-cultural venue not only
  for the students but also for the community. Behind is the annex, a
  concrete building that houses the new college canteen and the textbook
  center on the ground floor and the College library on the upper floor.

1988 - New Diocese & Religious of the Good Shepherd
  In 1988, Kabankalan was made a new Diocese with Most Reverend Vicente M.
  Navarra, D.D. as its First Bishop. That year, the College obtained the
  services of the Religious of the Good Shepherd who served for a year. For
  8 years the senior academic positions were taken by the lay members of the
  academic community.

1996 - Carmelite Sisters Invited
  In 1996, the Carmelite Sisters of Our Lady (Carm, O.L.) were invited by
  Most Rev. Vicente Navarra, D.D., Bishop of Kabankalan at the same time
  Chairman of the Board of Trustees, to assist Msgr. Rogelio B. Cruz, the
  School President, in the management of the school.

2003-2011 - Bishop Buzon's Era & New Leadership
  Most Reverend Patricio A. Buzon, SDB, D.D., was installed as the second
  Bishop of the Diocese of Kabankalan in 2003. In 2005, he administered the
  School as the Chairman of the Board of Trustees and as School President.
  Under his management, the KCC-Technological Center situated in Barangay
  Talubangi, Kabankalan City was established in 2009. In 2011, Rev. Fr.
  Radny T. Bayog, PhD, was appointed as School President of KCC.

2018-2019 - Bishop Galbines & First Alumnus President
  In 2018, Most Rev. Louie P. Galbines, SThD, D.D. was installed as the
  third Bishop of the Diocese of Kabankalan. He also served as the Chairman
  of the school's Board of Trustees. In 2019, Rev. Fr. Eugene D. Lucerna II,
  PhD, RPsy, RGC was appointed as School President. He was the first alumnus
  (KCC High School Batch 1996) to take over the aforementioned leadership
  position.

2025 - Fr. Richard L. Lachica Appointed
  On April 15, 2025, Bishop Galbines appointed Rev. Fr. Richard L. Lachica,
  MAEd, as the new school president starting Academic Year 2025-2026.

--- 2.6 THE SCHOOL SEAL ("Identity & Meaning" / "The Emblem, Decoded") ---
The Twelve Waves
  The twelve (12) waves at the rim of the logo represent the twelve (12)
  apostles tasked by Christ to teach and evangelize.
The Motto
  The school motto "TEACH YE ALL NATIONS" expresses KCC's untiring response
  to Christ's call for evangelization. Every KCCian should then strive to be
  a model Christian and true patriot. In this way, they will be honest and
  responsible citizens.
The Two Stars
  The two stars in the seal symbolize the guiding light of Catholic
  Education. One star represents KCC's identity, being rooted in Catholic
  faith and values. The other star represents its mission, to spread
  knowledge, truth, and service to others.
The Philippine Map
  The Philippine map, specifically the island of Negros, represents the
  immediate world KCC aims to evangelize. The other islands represent the
  world which KCC hopes to reach out to.
Dark Blue & Gold
  The dark blue and gold seal symbolizes wisdom, integrity, and excellence.
  Dark blue represents knowledge and moral strength, while gold embodies the
  eternal light of Christ, guiding the KCCians to success and dignity. This
  version highlights KCC's prestige and commitment to form graduates who
  embody truth and honor.

Founding as Kabankalan Institute
In 1927 a group of incorporators gathered to establish a secondary school named Kabankalan Institute which was later changed to Kabankalan Private Academy.
First Commencement & The War Years
The first commencement exercises of the school was in 1940. Classes were suspended during the Second World War (1941-1944) and were resumed in the school year 1945-1946. The Board of Trustees got a permit to open a Junior Normal College which lasted only for three years due to poor enrollment.
Columban Fathers Acquire the Academy
In 1953, the Columban Fathers through Fr. Thomas J. Cronin, parish priest of Kabankalan, thought of putting up a new school but later negotiated to buy Kabankalan Academy. However, most of the stockholders donated their shares in exchange for scholarship privileges.
Columban Era
Transfer to the Church Site & Directorship Practice
When the Columban Fathers took over, the first decision they made was to transfer the site of the school to the space near the church. The school directorship under the Columban Fathers was not a permanent position. The change of the directorship went with the reshuffling of the parish priest. This was the standing regulation among the Columban Fathers.
College & Elementary Departments Open
Through the untiring efforts of Fr. Cronin, a general college and an elementary education was opened in the school year 1963-1964. Two years after, he was able to get the services of the St. Paul Sisters of Chartres, to assist in the management of the school. Their experience in school management was an asset. Through them, Kabankalan College became the center of learning in the southern part of Negros Occidental.
Diocesan Administration Begins
The College department was built on a hectare of land donated by the Zayco family. In 1983 the management services of the Sisters of St. Paul terminated and the school administration shifted to the hands of the Diocesan priests headed by Msgr. Josefino I. Iledan as President and assisted by the Presentation Sisters.
The Great Fire & Reconstruction
On March 14, 1987, while the town was celebrating its 80th Foundation Day, a fire of dubious origin razed the high school building, the parish convent and the Old Catholic church to the ground. The catastrophe brought untold financial losses to the school. Massive rehabilitation and reconstruction were immediately instituted as the school faced the opening of the next school year. Meanwhile the high school department was temporarily relocated to the College campus while the permanent three-storey building on the newly acquired lot adjacent to the College was under construction.
Campus Expansion
St. Columban Arts Center & Annex
An additional phase to the physical structure of the college is the St. Columban Arts Center which serves as the socio-cultural venue not only for the students but also for the community. Behind is the annex, a concrete building that houses the new college canteen and the textbook center on the ground floor and the College library on the upper floor.
New Diocese & Religious of the Good Shepherd
In 1988, Kabankalan was made a new Diocese with Most Reverend Vicente M. Navarra, D.D. as its First Bishop. That year, the College obtained the services of the Religious of the Good Shepherd who served for a year. For 8 years the senior academic positions were taken by the lay members of the academic community.
Carmelite Sisters Invited
In 1996, the Carmelite Sisters of Our Lady (Carm, O.L.) were invited by Most Rev. Vicente Navarra, D.D., Bishop of Kabankalan at the same time Chairman of the Board of Trustees, to assist Msgr. Rogelio B. Cruz, the School President, in the management of the school.
Bishop Buzon's Era & New Leadership
Most Reverend Patricio A. Buzon, SDB, D.D., was installed as the second Bishop of the Diocese of Kabankalan in 2003. In 2005, he administered the School as the Chairman of the Board of Trustees and as School President. Under his management, the KCC-Technological Center situated in Barangay Talubangi, Kabankalan City was established in 2009. In 2011, Rev. Fr. Radny T. Bayog, PhD, was appointed as School President of KCC.
Bishop Galbines & First Alumnus President
In 2018, Most Rev. Louie P. Galbines, SThD, D.D. was installed as the third Bishop of the Diocese of Kabankalan. He also served as the Chairman of the school’s Board of Trustees. In 2019, Rev. Fr. Eugene D. Lucerna II, PhD, RPsy, RGC was appointed as School President. He was the first alumnus (KCC High School Batch 1996) to take over the aforementioned leadership position.
Fr. Richard L. Lachica Appointed
On April 15, 2025, Bishop Galbines appointed Rev. Fr. Richard L. Lachica, MAEd, as the new school president starting Academic Year 2025-2026.
Formation in Christ
Kabankalan Catholic College, Inc. is committed to forming socially conscious, responsible, and service-oriented individuals, guided by the teachings of Christ. They embody a profound understanding of the challenges and opportunities inherent in a rapidly evolving world.
Evangelizing Mission
As an evangelizing arm of the Diocese of Kabankalan, KCC seeks to proclaim and spread the teachings of the Church. The institution shepherds students and the community toward a deeper relationship with Christ; strengthening faith that transforms lives through service, compassion, and steadfast devotion to Christian values.
Preferential Option for the Poor
As a preferential option for the poor, Kabankalan Catholic College affirms that education is a universal right. Rooted in a curriculum that integrates the arts, sciences, and the Gospel, a KCC education fosters well-rounded development — one that nurtures each person's mind, body, and soul within a safe and supportive environment.
Academic Excellence
As an educational institution, KCC seeks academic excellence with a strong moral foundation characterized by integrity, compassion, and generosity. Our students and graduates are equipped with the skills, knowledge, and disposition necessary to contribute positively to society and to respond effectively to the needs of the Diocese, the nation, and the global community.

Board Exam Topnotcher
Kabankalan Catholic College, Inc. (KCC) celebrates another academic milestone after alumna Kristel Clair T. Paltu-ob, LPT secures a spot among the top examinees in the March 2026 Licensure Examination for Professional Teachers (LEPT).
Paltu-ob ranked eighth nationwide after earning a 93.20% rating in the board examination, bringing pride and recognition to the KCC community. Her achievement highlights years of dedication and commitment to the teaching profession, further reflecting the institution’s pursuit of academic excellence.
The college commends Paltu-ob for her perseverance and hard work, describing her accomplishment as both a personal success and a significant achievement for the school. KCC also expresses confidence that she will continue to make meaningful contributions in the field of education as she begins her professional journey as a licensed educator.
Her success now serves as an inspiration to aspiring teachers and students, showing that determination and discipline can lead to remarkable achievements on the national stage.
The KCC community extends its congratulations to Paltu-ob for bringing honor to the institution through her outstanding performance in the March 2026 LEPT.

Blessing Of The New CEAS Building
Through God’s abundant grace, Kabankalan Catholic College, Inc. joyfully celebrates the Blessing of the New CEAS Building today, January 16, 2026. This two-storey academic facility, featuring an integrated swimming pool, marks a significant milestone in the institution’s journey. It stands as a testament to KCC’s commitment to academic excellence, holistic formation, and the continuous enhancement of learning environments that nurture both mind and character.
The new CEAS Building reflects the shared vision, dedication, and generous support of the administration, faculty, staff, benefactors, and partners, as KCC continues to provide quality, values-driven education in service of the community and for the greater glory of God.
The blessing ceremony was presided over by Most Rev. Louie P. Galbines, SThD, DD, together with the KCC administrators and faculty.

KADICOSCA Updates
The KCC Men's Basketball team reclaimed championship title after dethroning defending champions, the BCC Stewards, in a nail-biting 75-69 victory held this morning, April 23, at the CCC covered court.
From the opening quarter to halftime, BCC Stewards displayed strong effort to keep their opponet at bay, controlling the tempo of the game with scores 25-13 and 38-32, respectively .
By third quarter, Knights mounted a strong comeback. Their relentless effort to regain the momentum secured them a seven-points advantage, 57-50.
In the final decisive quarter, both team traded baskets, but knights held their composure and sealed the game, 75-69.
This feat marks the Knights unwavering determination and fervent desire to bring the title back to their alma mater.
Congratulations, KCC Knights!

the surroundings and school facilities before and after the class) designed and
supervised by the Committee on Discipline.

undesirable and transfer credentials
are immediately issued.

sanctions.

be in the form of service or community work related to the offense committed. This
sanction is imposed
alone or coexistent with other sanctions.

Declaration of Duty
Kabankalan Catholic College, Inc. recognizes the worth and dignity of all
persons. It recognizes that children are reflective of God’s presence and requires
vigilance and responsiveness to their needs for care, safety and protection.
Situation of Responsibility
All children enrolled in Kabankalan Catholic College are the direct
responsibility of the school while they are in school, during school hours.
This responsibility continues when children are from the school-on-school
sponsored excursions, retreat camps and other similar activities. In such
circumstances the school faculty and staff or staff members in charge activities
bear primary responsibility for them.
Care
There is a universal right of all children to have their care near met. Children
have seven needs, which must be met for their healthy development. These care
needs are:
1. Physical Care - food, shelter, clothing, medical care and freedom from physical
harm.
2. Psychological Care - the need for a child to develop:
•Self-identity – as a unique person within cultural and community context.
•Self-worth-positive value of self.
•Self-capacity - an individual belief that the child can positively undertake education,
work and social tasks and actions in a socially acceptable and appropriate manner.
3. Emotional Care - the need for children to receive nurturance, love and affection
to enable them to develop appropriate emotions toward themselves and towards
others in society.
4. Cognitive Care – the need for children to receive cognitive and intellectual stimulation
through formal and informal education, to enable him/her to develop his/her learning and
thinking skills and contribute these skills and abilities towards the development of society.
5. Social Care - the need for a child to belong to valued groups within society, to
ensure a sense of social inclusion, social giving and social identity.
6. Spiritual Care - the need for a child to develop in an environment where God's
presence is considered normal accepted and to understand the sense of the
sacred and a presence personally within the child's own self, as well others; to
express worship and prayer; and to sense personal unity with the natural
environment, the sustainability of which is valued by the child.
7. Moral Care - the need for a child to learn and understand what is considered
"good" individual and social behavior, to enable the development of a social
community of engagement and compassion.
The school is committed to ensuring that its services actually facilitate the
meeting of children's seven care needs, the benchmark of a service care
environment for schools in the Diocese. The benchmark provides the school
faculty and staff with a means to assess and determine when a child may be
harmed or be at risk of harm.
20
Child maltreatment is deemed to have occurred when there is a violation of
one or more of the seven care needs.
Maltreatment
Child maltreatment is a term used to describe acts which harm children.
There are two types of child maltreatment. Child Abuse and Child Neglect.
Child Abuse consists of actions towards a child, which result in harm towards
a child. There are six types child abuse. Children are often exposed to multiple
forms of abuse. Abuse can occur through actual occurrence of the abuse or
through the threat of abuse.
1. Physical Abuse – occurs when a child's body is physically injured. This abuse may
occur through punching, throwing, slapping, shaking, strangling, kicking, poisoning,
drowning or suffocating (U.K Dept of Health, 1999,p5 ).
2. Psychological abuse -is sustained, repetitive, inappropriate behavior which
damages, or substantially reduces child's sense of self-identity, worth and efficacy
and therefore impacts upon the child's creative and developmental potential of
crucially important mental faculties and mental processes - these Include
intelligence, memory, perception, attention, language and moral developmental
(Meemeduma, 2005; extending O'Hagan's (2002), definition).
3. Emotional Abuse - is sustained, repetitive, and inappropriate emotional
responses to a child's need for love, nurturance care
4. Sexual Abuse - is the involvement of a child in so activity, either through active
involvement or observation. This may include but is not limited to the enticement
or threat to child to engage in an unlawful sexual activity, the exploitation of a child
in child prostitution and the use of a child in pornographic activities, performances
and materials
5. Witnessing family violence - is exposure to actual or threats of verbal and
physical violence between family and extended family members.
6. Commercial exploitation - is the use of children in work or other activities for
the significant commercial and noncommercial benefit of others. This includes but
is not limited to harmful child labor, child prostitution, and the exploitation of
children through pornography. (The Philippines Republic Act No. 7610, Section 9)
Child Neglect consists of actions where there is failure to appropriately meet the
care needs of a child. There are five types of child neglect. Children are often
exposed to multiple forms of neglect. Neglect can occur through the actual
occurrence of neglect or through the threat to neglect a child.
1. Physical Neglect - a failure to respond to a child's physical care needs for food,
shelter, clothing, and medical care.
2. Psychological Neglect - a failure to contribute towards the development of a
child's sense of identity, worth and capacity
3. Emotional Neglect - a failure to facilitate the development of secure emotional
attachment between a child and others. through the failure to provide a child with
love. nurturance and Care.
4. Cognitive Neglect - a failure to contribute towards a child learning of culturally
and socially appropriate knowledge skills.
5. Moral Neglect - a failure to develop a child capacity understand and enact good
and acceptable personal and behaviors.
When a child is maltreated, it is common that a child is both abused and
neglected.

D. Guidelines on the Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) for Students and
Faculty Members
Over the past decade, educators have increasingly adopted technology to
deliver information and instruction online, resulting in varied outcomes. While such
tools have expanded educators' reach and provided flexibility in teaching methods,
challenges persist related to differences in computer access and proficiency, as
well as increased feelings of isolation and the need for self-directed learning. The
emergence of Artificial Intelligence (AI)-generated text represents the latest
development in higher education, prompting us to reconsider the nature of learning
both now and in the future.
AI technology improves access to information and enables the handling of
complex tasks, data analysis, and decision-making with minimal human
intervention. Therefore, the central question when integrating AI and technology
into teaching and learning is: at what point does their use compromise the learning
process?
The mere utilization of AI and other technological resources does not
inherently obstruct learning; rather, it hinges on employing these tools while
upholding academic integrity. AI-generated content may lack accuracy or clarity,
potentially leading to misrepresented research, uncredited sources, and hindering
students' comprehension of the subject matter, thus undermining the educational
process.
Cheating constitutes a grave offense. It detrimentally impacts the academic
community by eroding academic integrity, fostering mistrust, and perpetuating
unfair competition. Kabankalan Catholic College (KCC) takes a firm stance against
cheating and imposes penalties as stated in the Student Daily Guide (Summary of
Offences and Maximum Penalties, p. 18). Violations encompass a range of actions
such as cheating on exams, plagiarism, unauthorized reuse of assignments,
improper use of electronic devices and the internet, unauthorized collaboration,
tampering with graded assignments, forgery, dishonesty, facilitating academic
misconduct, and engaging in unfair competition. Lack of awareness of these
regulations will not be accepted as an excuse.
Adherence to academic honesty is mandatory for all submitted work.
Consequently, the use of AI tools is discouraged by KCC due to several reasons:
Content generated by AI tools may be construed as automated plagiarism, as it
draws upon pre-existing texts from undisclosed sources without proper attribution.
AI models inherently harbor biases, as they are trained on limited datasets, thereby
perpetuating errors present in those sources rather than challenging them.
AI tools possess limitations such as the inability to engage in critical thinking
to evaluate criteria and the absence of abductive reasoning to make judgments
based on incomplete information.
Any breaches of this policy will be addressed in accordance with KCC’s
academic integrity guidelines. Penalties for violations may include
warning/reprimand, three-day suspension, disciplinary probation and dismissal. It
is incumbent upon students to familiarize themselves with the academic integrity
policy and ensure that their use of AI-based tools conforms to these regulations.
Students shall:
Use AI tools wisely and intelligently, with the goal of deepening critical
understanding of subject matter and to support learning.
23
l Understand the design intent, inner workings, data inputs, and restrictions of
particular AI tools, while being mindful of ethical considerations and mitigating
human risks and harmful consequences related to the use of such tools.
l Students are required to:
l Attribute credit to AI tools whenever utilized, even if solely for generating
ideas rather than producing usable text or illustrations.
l Include an appendix when incorporating AI tools in assignments, detailing: a)
the entire interaction, highlighting relevant sections; b) a description of the
specific AI tools used (e.g., ChatGPT private subscription version or DALL-E
free version); c) an explanation of how the AI tools were applied (e.g.,
generating ideas, phrasing, text components, argumentation, evidence,
conceptual maps, key concept illustrations, etc.); d) rationale for employing
AI tools (e.g., time-saving, overcoming writer’s block, stimulating thought,
managing stress, improving clarity, translation, experimentation, etc.).
l Refrain from using AI tools during in-class examinations or assignments
unless explicitly permitted and instructed.
l Prior to submission, utilize AI detection tools and originality checks to ensure
their work is not inadvertently flagged.
l Employ AI tools judiciously to deepen comprehension of subject matter and
bolster learning.
Faculty members are expected to:
l Familiarize themselves with the functionality of AI tools, including their

l Evaluate the work of students who declare no use of AI tools as the grading

l This policy acknowledges that:
l It relies on goodwill, fairness, and integrity.
l Instructors may opt for stricter constraints on AI tool usage, ensuring
transparency and fairness in grading.
l Disparities in accessibility to subscription versions of AI tools may necessitate
revisions to the policy as distinctions between subscription and free versions
become clearer.
The policy may undergo revisions in response to other policies and emerging
technological advancements in AI tools.

F. On Praying Before and After Classes, The Angelus and Regina Caeli, and
3 O’clock Prayer Habit
Start and end your classes with a prayer. Faculty members may lead or
assign student-prayer leaders to lead the class into prayer.
The Angelus is a Catholic devotion commemorating the Incarnation of Christ.
As with many Catholic prayers, the name Angelus is derived from its incipit-the first
few words of the text: Angelus Domini nuntiavit Maria ("The Angel of the Lord
declared unto Mary"). The devotion is practiced by reciting as versicle and
response three Biblical verses narrating the mystery, alternating with the prayer
"Hail Mary". The Angelus exemplifies a species of prayers called the "prayer of the
devotee". This 13th century Marian devotion is traditionally recited at 6 A.M., 12:00
P.M. and 6 P.M. during the ordinary time of the liturgical calendar.
During the Easter season, the words of the hymn "Regina Caeli" (Queen of
Heaven) are substituted for the Angelus prayer, traditionally recited at 6:00 A.M.,
12:00 P.M. and 6:00 P.M. While the authorship of the Regina Caeli is unknown,
the hymn has been traced back to the twelfth century. It was in Franciscan use,
after Compline, in the first half of the following century. There is a legend that says
St. Gregory the Great (d. 604) heard the first three lines chanted by angels on a
certain Easter morning in Rome while he walked barefoot in a great religious
procession and that the saint thereupon added the fourth line: "Ora pro nobis Deum.
Alleluia."
The Three O'clock Prayer was traditionally attributed to Father Chaminade.
A chronological analysis of the texts indicated that, at the initiative of Mile de
Lamourous, it was first used at the Misericorde (1801), and later, in the Association
26
of Adele (1804). However, by means of these two co-founders of Father
Chaminade, one can trace the origin of this prayer to the Carmelites. This prayer
is recited at 3:00 P.M.
At 12:00 P.M., 3:00 P.M. and 6:00 P.M., our school's public address (PA)
system will play the recorded prayers. Throughout the entire campus (offices,
classrooms, libraries, laboratories, canteen, gym, lobby, gazebos, security post,
and playground), every KCCian must stand in attention, head bowed, right hand
over the chest, and joins in praying the Angelus, Regina Caeli (during Easter
season), and 3 o'clock Prayer.
These prayers interrupt whatever task KCCians are in, just as the arrival of
Christ in the Incarnation interrupts the world. Far from being a distraction, the
welcome interruption of these prayers teaches a life-long lesson: no activity is more
important than prayer not work, not meetings, not study, not conversation, not
extracurricular activities, not even recess.
G. Conservation of Energy and Campus Cleanliness and Orderliness
Energy Conservation and Campus Cleanliness
1. Class Advisers: Classrooms are the primary responsibility of class advisers.
Class advisers must instruct students to conserve energy and maintain cleanliness
and order in the classrooms. Added to this, class advisers must monitor the
cleanliness and orderliness of classrooms on a daily basis, and see to it that
electric fans and LED TVs are turned off after use.
2. Students: Students must actively participate in energy conservation and in
keeping their classroom clean and orderly. They should turn off fans and LED TVs
after using them, dispose of trash properly, arrange armchairs, refrain from
vandalizing armchairs and walls, and using any types of tapes on walls or furniture.
Waste Disposal
1. Recycling: KCCians must know the importance and benefits of recycling to the
environment. Thus, it is mandatory that students, faculty and staff must practice
recycling such as the use of scratch papers, plastic, tin and glass containers, and
to properly dispose recyclable materials in the recycling bins.
2. Trash Bins: Trash bins are provided in every floor for the proper disposal of nonrecyclable waste. It is the responsibility of both students and faculty members to
ensure that trash is placed in the correct bins and not left on the classroom floor,
armchairs or lobby.
Personal Belongings
1. Personal Responsibility: Students are responsible for their personal belongings
and should organize them in order to minimize clutter in the classroom. In the event
where students have to leave the classroom in order to do performance tasks,
attend Mass and prayer service, and other official school activities, students must
bring their personal belongings such as wallets, laptops, tablets, cellphones, and
other gadgets with them.
2. Lost and Found: The Office of the Discipline Officer is the in-charge of all "Lost
and Found" items. Students must check this office if they misplace any belongings.
Consequences for Non-Compliance
1. Failure to comply with the guidelines on campus and classroom cleanliness may
result to appropriate disciplinary actions as per the school's existing policies
27
outlined in the Student Daily Guide (Summary of Offences and Maximum Penalties,
pp. 18-19).
2. Vandalism, removal of wall paint due to sticking of tapes, or destruction of any
school property shall be charged accordingly (Summary of Offences and Maximum
Penalties, pp. 18-19).
H. Stakeholders’ Right Against Unreasonable Searches and Seizures
If the occasion warrants, authorized personnel of the school can validly conduct
search and seizures of stakeholders. Except for the following instances, no
searches and seizures shall be deemed valid:
a. Searches made at the point of entrance and exit in the campus and in the
buildings of the school by authorized personnel of the school.
b. Searches and seizures of the illegal articles as defined by the Revised
Penal Code and other related penal laws, Student Daily Guide and Administrative
and Employee Manual.
c. Searches and seizures of articles by duly authorized personnel (open to
his/her naked eye and hand) as evidence of a crime, contraband, or is otherwise
subject to seizure.
d. Searches made when any stakeholder is attempting to commit, is
committing, has just committed, or has been committing a crime or a serious
infraction of the school's rules and regulations.
e. Searches made with a valid search warrant.
f. Searches under any circumstance conducted on reasonable grounds.
